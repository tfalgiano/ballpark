# The 30-second name test

A human test. **No AI stands in for a person here** — the entire value is that
real ears hear a real voice once and produce a real spelling. A simulated version
of this would be me marking my own homework.

Target: **20 people.** Friends, family, colleagues, a queue, anyone. They do not
need to be gamers or know what you're building.

---

## Before you start — how to avoid poisoning it

The threat to this test is priming, and it is easy to do by accident.

- **Do not** say "I'm renaming my game" or "I'm testing a name."
- **Do not** show them anything written, on paper or a screen.
- **Do not** spell it, or say it twice, or repeat it if asked.
- **Do not** say "half… sure" with a pause. Say it as one word, at normal speed,
  the way you would say *"halfway"* or *"halfhearted"*.
- **Do not** react to their answer. No "close!", no wincing, no laughing. The
  next person in the room is your next data point.
- **Do not** test both names on the same person. The first one primes the second.
  Use different people for Nearbouts.

---

## The script

> **You:** Quick one — can I say a word at you and get your gut reaction?
>
> *(wait for yes)*
>
> **You:** Halfsure.
>
> **You:** If that were a website, what would you type into the address bar?
>
> *(write down EXACTLY what they say or spell — verbatim, including any hesitation)*
>
> **You:** And in two or three words — what does it make you think of?
>
> *(write it down verbatim)*
>
> **You:** That's it. Thanks.

If they ask you to repeat it, say **"just go with what you heard"** and record
whatever they produce. A name that needs repeating has already told you
something.

## The decision rule — precommitted, before any data exists

**PASS: ≥ 70% produce a spelling that finds us.**

That means, out of 20 people, **14 or more** writing any of:

- `halfsure`
- `half sure`
- `Half Sure` / `HalfSure` / `half-sure` — capitalisation and the space are
  immaterial, because search engines collapse them and both strings are
  effectively empty on the indexed web

**Does NOT count:** `halfshore`, `half shore`, `halfsur`, `halfsher`, `alfsure`,
or anything else. Those land nowhere.

**If it passes:** the central objection in the case against Halfsure was wrong,
and the name is confirmed.

**If it fails (13 or fewer):** the objection was right and I was wrong. The
recommendation flips to Nearbouts — *provided* Nearbouts scores materially
better on the same test. If Nearbouts also fails, neither name survives spoken
transmission and we would need a third round with spellability as a hard gate
rather than a weighting.

I am recording this threshold now, before the data exists, so it cannot be
adjusted afterwards to protect a conclusion. **70% was proposed before the first
person was asked and will not move.**
